What these papers do, and what they don't. A person becomes a managing member of an LLC through the company's operating agreement (and, in some states, its articles of organization or a filed statement of authority) — not through a certificate. The forms in this packet are the standard way an LLC evidences that existing authority to a bank, title company, insurer, lender, or vendor. They carry weight because the company's members sign them under oath, before a notary, and become responsible for what they state.

Sign these only if the facts stated in them are true and are backed by the operating agreement. If Nichilad Jaime is not yet designated as managing member in that agreement, amend the agreement first — then sign these.

What's in the packet

  1. Unanimous Written Consent & Resolution of the Members — the core document. The members of the company resolve and confirm that Nichilad Jaime is the Managing Member and spell out what he or she is authorized to do. This is what most banks and lenders ask for.
  2. Certificate of Incumbency and Authority — a one-page certification, with a specimen signature, that identifies who holds which office. Banks use it to match the signature on file.
  3. Statement of Authority — the version used for real estate and recording. In states that follow the Revised Uniform LLC Act, this is filed with the Secretary of State (and recorded with the county for real property) and gives constructive notice to the public.
  4. Certification Letter — a short letterhead letter for the counterparties that just need something in the file.

Before you print

  1. Confirm the exact legal spelling of the name against a government-issued ID and the operating agreement. The packet is typed as NICHILAD JAIME; if the agreement or ID differs by even a letter, correct every page before signing.
  2. Confirm the exact registered name of the entity, including the suffix, as shown on the Secretary of State's record — OTL Rental LLC.
  3. Fill in the state of formation, the date of the operating agreement, and the entity file/EIN number where blanks appear.
  4. List every current member on the signature pages. A consent signed by fewer than all members is not unanimous and banks will reject it.

Signing

  1. Print single-sided on letter paper. Use blue or black ink.
  2. Do not sign until you are in front of the notary — the notary must witness the signature.
  3. Bring photo ID for every signer.
  4. Keep the signed originals in the company's records book. Give counterparties copies, or notarized duplicate originals if they insist.
  5. Most banks also want: the filed articles of organization, the operating agreement, the EIN letter, and ID for each signer. Bring them.

One caveat

Requirements for statements of authority, notarial wording, and what a bank will accept vary by state. Have a licensed attorney in the state of formation review this packet before you rely on it for anything significant — a loan, a property transfer, or a lease.

Statement of Authority

For filing with the Secretary of State and, where the Company holds real property, for recording with the county recorder

Read before filing. In states that have adopted the Revised Uniform Limited Liability Company Act, a statement of authority gives public notice of a person’s authority only once it is filed with the Secretary of State; to affect real property, a certified copy must also be recorded in the county where the property sits. Filing fees, form numbers, and required wording differ by state — check the Secretary of State’s office for the state of formation, and use its official form if it requires one. A statement filed here generally lapses after five years unless cancelled or renewed sooner.
